Part A · The shape of an item

Every item carries a risks section, because the recorded failure is the fix that became the defect

Three regressions in this package came from a number whose direction was never stated. A page-fill floor was withdrawn because it could be satisfied without improving a page.

What to notice

  • Acceptance is written as something a machine or a reader can check, never as an intention.
  • The risks section names how this specific proposal could go the same way as the withdrawn floor.

Read the risks section first on any item you intend to build. It is the part that says what the item costs when it is done badly, which is the only cost that ever surprises anyone.

Part A · Claim classes

What kind of claim is this, and how much do you have to take on trust?

An internal analysis labels what it measured, what it asserts from the build, and what it inferred. Mixing the three is how a document becomes unarguable.

How to use the labels

  • A measured claim can be re-counted from the repository today; the count is printed beside it.
  • A build-time assertion is re-runnable: a command produces it, and a different machine gets the same answer.
  • An inference is a judgement. Disagreeing with one costs nothing and changes the ranking, not the evidence.

Nothing in Part B or Part C is an inference about whether a gap exists. The inferences are all about what it is worth, which is the part you should push back on.

Part B · Item 1, the problem

6 of 12 prose metrics have no code, and 3 of them carry a fact red line

The rubric heads its table “scriptable; spot-check manually when no script”. That parenthesis is carrying half the table.

measured · counted from check_prose.py

  • M1 is the only measurement behind the title contract's demand that a title carry a verifiable fact, and the changelog records a release whose titles collapsed to bare antitheses while it went unmeasured.
  • M2 and M6 stand behind the rule that every number carries its source, which the writing rules place above every style rule.

A deliverable can break all three today and pass every check this package ships.

Part B · Item 1, the proposal

Build M6, then M2, then M1 — cheapest predicate first, hardest judgement last

Each is independently shippable. The order is by how decidable the predicate is, not by how much the rule matters.

inference · the ordering is a judgement

  • M6 is nearly written: the range pattern already exists in the checker and is used only to exempt ranges from the dash rule.
  • M1 has no decidable predicate. A workable proxy is a title containing a numeral, a named entity or a dated term, printed with its misses.

M1 ships reported, not gating, until two releases of real documents have been read against it. A title heuristic that fires on good titles is a line reviewers learn to skip.

Part B · Item 1, the risk

Why does M1 report instead of gate, when M2 and M6 fail the run?

A metric that gates gets satisfied. If M1 gates on a regex, authors write titles the regex likes, and the package has done this to itself before.

Acceptance, per metric

  • Each appears in the grade table with a target and in the fixture expectations for both fixtures.
  • Each has a failing case planted in the broken fixture. A metric asserted ok on both cannot tell a working checker from one rewritten to return ok.
  • M2 and M6 fail the run. M1 reports.

Adding a metric adds a copy of a rule. Whatever M2 counts as a source marker has to be stated in the writing rules and held to them, or the two drift the way the ban list once did.

Part B · Item 2, the problem

Chinese is a thing this package detects and excludes, not a thing it produces well

The rules cover Chinese output across four sections. The machinery covers none of it. The only Chinese string CI touches is the negative case that makes a metric fail.

measured · counted across rules, scripts and fixtures

  • The prose checker states in its own docstring that it handles English deliverables only.
  • The ban-list parity guard covers the English list. The Chinese list has no machine counterpart, so it can drift with nothing noticing.
  • No Chinese fixture exists. Both tracked fixtures are English.

No display face ships for Chinese. The rules require the face to be embedded rather than linked, and a Chinese deliverable falls back to whatever the reader's machine has.

Part B · Item 2, the proposal

Three phases: parity first, then a checker, then a fixture

Phase one is worth doing even if the other two never happen, because it stops the rules and the machine from separating any further.

What each phase buys

  • Parity first. Extend the ban-list guard to the Chinese list under the same discipline: every banned phrase is a pattern or a documented exemption.
  • Then a checker path. Punctuation and term consistency are mechanizable. The de-translationese pass is judgement about register and should be recorded as not mechanized rather than approximated.
  • Then a fixture pair, so the Chinese metrics have a failing case like everything else.
  • Sentence-rhythm segmentation stays out: it does not transfer from English, and no defect has asked for it.

Phase one is a guard rather than a feature, which is why it is first: it costs least and it is the only one of the three that stops the gap widening while the others wait.

Part B · Item 2, the blocker

Why is a font licence the blocker, and not any amount of engineering time?

The Latin face is vendored under a licence that permits embedding. A Chinese face of comparable quality may not be, and it is one to two orders of magnitude larger.

Two honest answers, and one dishonest one

  • Vendor a face whose licence permits embedding, and accept what it does to the size of every deliverable that carries it.
  • State the fallback plainly in the design rules: Chinese deliverables use system faces, and here is what that costs on a machine that lacks them.
  • Say nothing, which is the current position and the one the maintenance rules already forbid.

A rule may not mandate an asset the package does not ship. That rule was written about an icon library and a display face; it applies to a language exactly as well.

Part C · Item 3, the proposal

Plant a failure for every metric, then bring the renderer into the suite

Two moves in order, and the first decomposes into one metric at a time so it can be done between other work.

Acceptance

  • Every emitted verdict has at least one fixture on which it fails.
  • The fixture runner reports which gates it asserted and which it could not, and the count it could not is zero where a browser is present.

The broken fixture is also teaching material. A fixture carrying every defect at once stops being readable as an example, so keep one defect per page and labelled, or split a third fixture whose only job is to fail.

Part C · Item 4

The iteration engine: six dimensions, a protocol, and nothing stored

Six human dimensions, a protocol where a divergence of two forces a retrospective, and not one stored score. Every score in the record is a sentence inside a release note.

The proposal, and the guard it needs

  • One tracked file: release, document genre, six self-scores, six reader scores, retrospective outcome. A short command prints each dimension over time.
  • Backfill the scores already sitting in the changelog as prose.

This is where engagement facts leak into the repository. A scores file is exactly the shape that breaks the rule against client names, so the guard is not optional and the schema gets no free-text field that invites one.

Part C · Item 5

2 viewport checks the rules require, and nothing runs them by default

A short laptop window and a full-HD stage are both defined in the renderer and both absent from its default list, while the design rules name the first one and say what it catches.

What to do

  • Add both to the default geometry list, or state in the rules that they are opt-in and why. Prefer the first.
  • The argument for the off-geometry render is that a defect invisible at the design size shows at a shape nobody designed for. That argument covers these two exactly as it covers the one that does run.

If two more renders per run cost too much, make them default under the delivery flag and optional otherwise. Dropping them is the one answer the rules already argue against.

Part C · Item 6

Three capability tiers are defined and two are used, and the unused one is the shape of every IDE

The registry holds ten platforms that run scripts and two that receive a pasted prompt. Nothing sits on the tier that reads the rules and cannot execute.

Decide which it is

  • Either a real platform belongs there, in which case add the record, the loading note and the verification wording that tier implies.
  • Or the tier is aspirational, and the registry should say so in a waiver like the ones it already carries.
  • Worth testing first: some of the ten may not execute scripts in every mode, which would make the registry optimistic rather than the tier unused.
  • Whichever way it goes, the verification wording changes with it: a tier that cannot execute may not call a deliverable verified.

Adding a platform without confirming its behaviour is the failure the manifest guard exists to prevent. One record already carries three waivers because its discovery path could not be confirmed; do not add a fourth by guessing.

Part C · Item 7

1 agent of 12 on the board, one run each, and an unscored run on disk

Ten rows read “not installed”, but four of those can never be exercised at all: an IDE with no command line, and two chat models behind an API.

Three parts, cheapest first

  1. Prune or score the orphan run. A results directory no table cites is a claim nobody made.
  2. Separate cannot from not-yet. Ten rows look the same; only six are ever actionable.
  3. Repeat a run. The board's own note says a verdict can change without the artifact changing.

Until a run repeats, every row is one sample and the board cannot tell a flaky checker from a flaky agent. Repeating costs tokens and produces an uncomfortable number, which is the value.

Part D · Already landed

Three findings were small enough to fix while the survey was being written

All three are recorded here rather than proposed, and the third was found by this document: a figure of its own rendered half its rows while every check stayed green.

build-time assertion · shipped at 0.1.385 and 0.1.386

  • An unknown genre in a document's markup was graded against the sales target in silence. A misspelling scored a training handbook against the wrong number and printed a confident line about it. It now reports as not measured and names the vocabulary it knows.
  • The delivery flag's help text listed eight findings after a ninth had shipped.
  • A viewBox with three numbers instead of four is legal as an attribute and meaningless as a value. The browser discards it; the clipping probe read the unparsed box as nothing to measure and skipped it. Tenth gate, with a planted case.

The second is documentation describing behaviour, and nothing in this package compares the two. That is the same class of defect as a metric with no failing case.
